# Requirements

## Core requirements
1. Define S∞ and B∞ mathematically without assuming physical ontology.
2. Define the local-to-global map explicitly.
3. Define relational structure and its response operator.
4. Define dynamics rather than relying on analogies.
5. State boundary conditions and conserved quantities.
6. Use a controlled finite-N formulation before an infinity limit.
7. Specify the N→∞ / continuum procedure.
8. Distinguish coordinate labels from physical observables.
9. Define any proposed effective distance/time independently.
10. Permit explicit falsification.

## Causality
Test finite-speed propagation, causal cones, locality, stability and well-posedness. Earlier parent work found that simple first-order diffusion/drift does not automatically provide relativistic finite-speed propagation.

## Quantum
Eventually address Hilbert-space structure, normalization, consistent evolution, superposition, interference, measurement/observables, and known quantum limits. Normal modes alone are insufficient.

## Geometry / GR
Eventually address an independently derived metric, Lorentzian signature, causal structure, geodesic behavior, equivalence-principle tests, conservation/Bianchi consistency, Newtonian limit, gravitational waves, and Einstein dynamics without inserting GR by hand.

## Thermodynamics
Keep as a future branch. Eventually derive or explain microstates, coarse-graining, entropy, temperature, equilibrium, irreversibility, entropy production and arrow of time.

## Electromagnetism / charge / current
Keep separate until candidate conserved/gauge-like structures arise naturally. Do not equate K, antisymmetry, circulation, phase or any other variable with charge without derivation.

## Theory protection
For every proposed identification ask: What is the mathematical variable? What established quantity is it compared with? What derivation connects them? What benchmark must be recovered? What would falsify it?

## Minimum survival standard
A coherent formulation, nontrivial dynamics, controlled continuum limit, causal structure, quantum-limit test, geometry/GR-limit test, and at least one nontrivial quantitative prediction not inserted by construction.
