# Analogy A020 — Plasma / Wick: Distributed Energetic Flow → Localized Manifestation

**Conceptual source:** Two physical observations used together as an analogy:  
(1) a hot plasma as a distributed, dynamically active many-body state in which energy transfer, radiation, ionization and recombination change the organization of the state;  
(2) a liquid reservoir feeding a localized flame through a porous wick, where capillary transport and local reaction/heat-transfer processes determine the visible manifestation.

## 📖 The observations

### 1. Plasma

A plasma is an ionized state containing charged constituents such as electrons and ions. Plasma dynamics can involve energy input, transport, radiation, recombination, and changes in charge-state populations.

The research analogy does **not** use "plasma" to mean an unknown substance underlying spacetime. It uses the more general structural feature:

> a distributed energetic many-body state can undergo constrained redistribution and change its organization.

NASA educational and technical material describes plasma as an ionized state and discusses recombination and heating/cooling processes in plasma systems.

### 2. Ghee / wick / flame

The everyday observation is that ghee or another liquid fuel can be supplied through a fibrous wick and, at the exposed tip, participate in a localized flame that produces visible light and heat.

The physical wick mechanism is capillary transport through a porous material followed by vaporization and combustion near the flame region.

The research analogy extracts only:

> distributed supply + constrained transport pathway + localized transformation → localized observable manifestation.

The wick itself is **not** identified with spacetime.

## 💡 Why this analogy was proposed

The analogy sharpens a problem already present in **1.12.6 Matter / Particle Excitations**.

Earlier work established that an assumed relational update law can move a localized disturbance through a relational structure, but it did not answer the deeper question:

> **How can a localized object arise in the first place without inserting the object into the model?**

The new analogy suggests that the correct starting point may be a **distributed active state**, rather than a pre-existing localized particle.

## 🧪 Testable component

The analogy should be converted into the following test:

> **Can an initially distributed relational state spontaneously develop a localized, persistent configuration under generic relational constraints, without specifying the final object beforehand?**

A minimal toy model may use relational degrees of freedom \(x_i\), couplings \(K_{ij}\), and a local potential \(V(x_i)\):

\[
E[x]
=
\frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2
+
\sum_iV(x_i).
\]

The initial condition should be broadly distributed rather than containing a localized "particle":

\[
e_i(0)\approx e_{\rm broad}.
\]

The target is a dynamically generated localized excess,

\[
e_i(t)
=
e_i^{\rm background}
+
\Delta e_i(t),
\]

with \(\Delta e_i(t)\) becoming localized and remaining sufficiently persistent.

The exact dynamics, potential, coupling structure, and conserved quantity must be independently motivated. They must not be chosen merely because they produce a particle-like result.

### Required success conditions

A candidate localized structure should satisfy several conditions together:

1. **Emergence** — it forms dynamically rather than being inserted.
2. **Localization** — its defining relational activity remains spatially/effectively localized.
3. **Persistence** — it survives for a substantial number of updates.
4. **Identity** — an invariant or stable descriptor can track it.
5. **Perturbation response** — small disturbances do not immediately destroy it.
6. **Propagation/interaction** — where the dynamics permits, it can move or interact while retaining identity.
7. **Representation robustness** — its identity survives reasonable coarse-graining/relabeling.
8. **Parameter robustness** — the phenomenon is not a single hand-tuned numerical accident.
9. **Energy/accounting consistency** — localization does not rely on an untracked energy source or sink.

## 🔬 Known-science connection

The analogy draws on established physics, but the proposed S∞/B∞ mapping is not established.

### Plasma

NASA material describes plasma as an ionized state and discusses energy balance, recombination, and radiative processes. These provide a real example in which distributed energetic dynamics and interaction processes can change the organization of matter.

### Wick

NIST defines a wick as material that draws an ignitable liquid by capillary action. NASA candle-flame work likewise models liquid-fuel transport through a porous wick and subsequent vaporization/combustion near the flame.

These facts support the **structural analogy only**. They do not support the claim that spacetime is a plasma or wick.

## ⚠️ What this analogy does NOT explain

It does not by itself derive:

- S∞ or B∞;
- physical spacetime;
- physical vacuum energy;
- particle mass;
- charge;
- spin;
- quantum statistics;
- the Standard Model;
- gravity;
- photons;
- electromagnetism;
- a universal action scale;
- physical time.

In particular:

\[
\text{plasma} \neq B_\infty
\]

and

\[
\text{particle} \neq \text{cooled plasma}.
\]

The analogy is about **organization under constraints**, not about literal material identity.

## 🪞 Non-testable / metaphorical component

The following language is metaphorical unless independently formalized:

- "energy flowing underneath matter";
- "the object pulls energy away from the background";
- "spacetime is a wick";
- "particles condense out of plasma";
- "light is the flame at the end of the cosmic wick."

\[
\text{microscopic relational activity}\neq0

\text{macroscopic configuration change}\approx0.
\]

That is, a stable object could remain dynamically active while its macroscopic identity is approximately unchanged.

### Explicit failure modes

- localization is only a transient pulse;
- localization exists only because the initial state already contains it;
- an externally imposed trap creates the "particle";
- dissipation or cooling is added without a physical/relational basis;
- localization occurs only under arbitrary fine tuning;
- apparent persistence is merely a finite simulation-time artifact;
- no invariant identity can be assigned to the localized structure.

### Decision rule

If the test fails, retain A020 as an **illustrative analogy** but do not use it as support for emergent particle formation.

If it succeeds under generic, independently motivated relational rules, record the result as a **toy-level structural lead** for 1.12.6. It still would not establish a physical particle without further derivation and calibration.
