## 1. Purpose

This branch tests a specific question arising from the S∞ ↔ B∞ relational hypothesis:

> Can two operationally isolated systems remain correlated because their observable outcomes are generated from a shared relational state, rather than from independent local predetermined instruction tables?

The target is not merely to obtain correlation. The model must address all of the following:

1. A and B remain operationally isolated.
2. The correlation is generated by a shared relational state.
3. The model reproduces the singlet target
   \[
   E(a,b)=-\cos(a-b).
   \]
4. The resulting correlations violate the Bell/CHSH local bound.
5. The observable marginals remain independent of the remote setting (no-signalling).

A Bell violation alone is not treated as proof of S∞ ↔ B∞.

## 3. EPR / Bell Baseline — EXP-BELL-01

EPR asks whether quantum mechanics can be complete while retaining locality, or whether additional underlying information is required.

Bell converts this into a testable constraint: local hidden-variable models with predetermined local responses satisfy Bell inequalities, while quantum mechanics predicts correlations that can violate them.

The important distinction for this research is:

**Bell does not prohibit correlation. It constrains the structure of correlations obtainable from local predetermined response functions.**

Therefore the S∞ ↔ B∞ hypothesis must not simply reproduce correlation; it must avoid silently reintroducing a local instruction table.

**Status:** 🟢 baseline established.

## 4. X–Z–Q Boolean Configuration — EXP-BELL-02

For three binary variables/settings X, Z, Q, the configuration space contains

\[
2^3=8
\]

possible assignments.

The Bell counting argument can be viewed as a parity structure over these binary assignments: for three predetermined binary values, the number of pairwise disagreements among (X,Z), (Z,Q), and (X,Q) is necessarily even.

This is the combinatorial skeleton behind the familiar Bell counting argument.

**Status:** 🟢 structural result.

## 6. Continuous Local Hidden-Variable Test — EXP-BELL-04

A continuous local hidden variable was tested using the representative threshold construction

\[
A=\operatorname{sign}[\cos(a-\lambda)],
\]

with the corresponding anti-correlated local response for B.

For uniformly distributed \(\lambda\), the normalized correlation becomes

\[
E_{local}(\theta)=-1+\frac{2\theta}{\pi},
\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi,
\]

rather than

\[
-\cos\theta.
\]

The resulting model remains within the local Bell/CHSH bound.

### Result

**Continuity by itself does not solve Bell's problem.**

This closes the simplistic route:

> continuous hidden variable → quantum cosine.

**Status:** 🔴 failed target; retained as a negative result.

## 7. Shared Continuous Relational-State Target — EXP-BELL-05

A target joint distribution was specified:

\[
P(A,B|a,b)=\frac14\left[1-AB\cos(a-b)\right],
\qquad A,B\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

This gives

\[
E(a,b)=\langle AB\rangle=-\cos(a-b).
\]

The construction has uniform local marginals:

\[
P(A=+1|a,b)=P(A=-1|a,b)=\frac12,
\]

and similarly for B, so the marginal statistics do not depend on the remote setting.

### Important protection

This distribution was **constructed as the mathematical target**. It was not derived from Chandas or from S∞ ↔ B∞.

Therefore:

**target achieved mathematically ≠ physical derivation achieved.**

**Status:** 🟡 target construction / benchmark.

## 8. Why cos θ Appears — EXP-CHANDAS-02 / EXP-BELL-05

For a continuous phase variable with a uniform phase,

\[
\left\langle\cos\phi\cos(\phi+\theta)\right\rangle
=\frac12\cos\theta.
\]

After normalization, the phase correlation is

\[
C(\theta)=\cos\theta.
\]

The singlet sign convention gives

\[
E(\theta)=-\cos\theta.
\]

This is a genuine mathematical mechanism: **relative phase of a common periodic mode naturally generates cosine correlation.**

However, the difficult step remains the conversion from the continuous/multimode relational state into the required binary measurement distribution without simply imposing the Bell target.

**Status:** 🟢 harmonic mechanism; binary-outcome derivation remains open.

## 9. Bell / CHSH Test — EXP-BELL-06

For the standard angular choices

\[
a=0^\circ,\quad a'=90^\circ,\quad
b=45^\circ,\quad b'=-45^\circ,
\]

the singlet correlation gives

\[
|S|=2\sqrt2\approx2.828.
\]

The local CHSH bound is

\[
|S|\le2.
\]

Therefore the target correlation violates the local Bell/CHSH bound.

This is a mathematical consequence of the imposed \(-\cos\theta\) correlation, not yet a derived consequence of Chandas or S∞ ↔ B∞.

**Status:** 🟢 mathematical target result.

## 10. No-Signalling Test — EXP-BELL-07

For

\[
P(A,B|a,b)=\frac14[1-AB\cos(a-b)],
\]

summing over B gives

\[
P(A|a,b)=\frac12,
\]

independent of b.

Similarly,

\[
P(B|a,b)=\frac12,
\]

independent of a.

Thus the target distribution is non-signalling.

The research requirement is therefore stronger than simply obtaining Bell violation:

**Bell violation + no-signalling must coexist.**

**Status:** 🟢 mathematical target result.

## 12. Raw Chandas vs Pure Cosine — EXP-CHANDAS-03/04/05

The working audit reached the following methodological boundary:

- A periodic discrete Chandas pattern can possess spectral/harmonic structure.
- A general raw metrical pattern is not automatically a pure sinusoid.
- Higher harmonics can contribute to its correlation function.
- Selecting or extracting a first harmonic mathematically produces cosine correlation.

Therefore:

**Raw Chandas → pure cos θ is NOT established as automatic.**

The first-harmonic route is mathematically valid but does not by itself explain why a physical measurement should select that harmonic.

This is a crucial negative/limiting result and must remain in the research record.

**Status:** 🔴 for the claim “raw Chandas naturally equals pure cosine”; 🟢 for the mathematical first-harmonic mechanism.

## 16. Research Protection / What Has NOT Been Proven

- Piṅgala discovered Bell's theorem.
- Chandas is quantum mechanics.
- Chandas is physically entangled.
- A periodic wave by itself violates Bell's inequality.
- Continuity by itself defeats Bell's theorem.
- A cosine correlation by itself proves S∞ ↔ B∞.
- Bell violation alone proves the S∞ ↔ B∞ substrate.
- A constructed no-signalling distribution is a physical derivation.
- A Chandas first harmonic is a photon, particle, or physical field.

The branch is valuable precisely because these distinctions are explicit.
