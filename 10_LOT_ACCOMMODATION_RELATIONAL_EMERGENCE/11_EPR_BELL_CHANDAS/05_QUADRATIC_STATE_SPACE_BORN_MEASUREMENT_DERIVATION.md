# PGA 1.12.2.10.15 — Derivation of Quantum State Space and Probability/Measurement from Meru Quadratic Projection

## Objective

Attempt to derive the quantum state-space and measurement/probability rules directly from the same Meru quadratic projection, without starting by postulating Hilbert space, the Born rule, or projector algebra.

## 1. Fundamental quadratic map

For a normalized pair

  a²+b²=1,

Meru supplies

  a², 2ab, b².

Define

  X=a²-b²,
  Y=2ab.

Then

  X²+Y²=1.

Thus the quadratic projection maps normalized two-component data to a circle.

This result is the starting object, not Hilbert space.

## 2. Composition of quadratic modes

Introduce N independent binary relational channels. Their Prastāra configurations are indexed by binary strings s∈{0,1}^N.

Meru generalizes from the binomial row to the multinomial grouping of configurations by occupation counts. The quadratic two-component map can be applied pairwise to each independent mode.

A general normalized relational state can therefore be represented by a collection of complex pairs

  z_k = X_k+iY_k,

with

  |z_k|²=X_k²+Y_k².

The global normalization candidate is

  Σ_k |z_k|²=1.

This is the first point at which a complex vector space appears from repeated quadratic projections.

## 3. Why the complex field is selected

The quadratic pair (X,Y) has a norm-preserving rotation:

  (X,Y) → (X cosφ-Y sinφ, X sinφ+Y cosφ).

The algebraic object z=X+iY converts this rotation into multiplication by e^{iφ}.

Thus complex phase is the minimal algebraic representation of the continuous symmetry generated by the Meru quadratic circle.

This does NOT yet establish that complex numbers are the unique possible field for all physical states. It establishes a natural candidate.

## 4. State-space construction

Let the distinguishable relational outcome classes be indexed by i.

Associate one complex amplitude z_i with each class.

Define

  |ψ) := (z_1,z_2,...,z_n),

with

  Σ_i |z_i|²=1.

At this stage the notation is deliberately not called a Hilbert vector. It is a normalized complex amplitude array generated from quadratic relational modes.

The inner product is then introduced as the unique standard sesquilinear invariant candidate compatible with phase rotations of each component:

  (φ|ψ)=Σ_i φ_i* ψ_i.

This is a candidate derivation, but uniqueness has not been proven from S∞↔B∞.

## 5. Measurement as quadratic coarse-graining

A measurement partitions relational configurations into mutually exclusive outcome classes.

For a class C, define its amplitude by coherent aggregation:

  z_C = Σ_{i∈C} z_i

when phase relations are retained.

The quadratic weight is

  W(C)=|z_C|².

For mutually exclusive orthogonal/coherently separated classes, cross terms vanish and

  W(C)=Σ_{i∈C}|z_i|².

Normalization gives

  P(C)=W(C)/Σ_D W(D).

For a normalized state:

  P(C)=W(C).

This produces the Born form from the same quadratic projection.

## 6. Binary measurement

For two complementary outcomes,

  P_+ = |z_+|²,
  P_- = |z_-|²,

and

  P_+ + P_-=1.

The observable binary mean is

  <A>=P_+-P_-.

For the Meru circle,

  X=a²-b²=P_+-P_-.

Therefore the expectation coordinate is exactly the first quadratic projection coordinate.

The second coordinate Y=2ab carries the phase/interference component.

This gives a direct bridge:

  Meru quadratic projection
      → outcome weights
      → binary expectation
      → phase/interference coordinate.

## 7. Projection geometry

A measurement basis corresponds to a decomposition into mutually exclusive relational outcome directions.

The probability for an outcome is the squared magnitude of the corresponding projected amplitude.

For a normalized two-dimensional state, changing the measurement basis rotates the quadratic circle. Relative rotation gives

  P_+ - P_- = cosθ

and

  P_+ = cos²(θ/2),
  P_- = sin²(θ/2).

These familiar formulas are now downstream coordinate expressions of the quadratic geometry rather than initial axioms.

## 8. General probability rule

For an outcome subspace represented by a projection map M_C,

  P(C)=||M_C ψ||² / ||ψ||².

If the state is normalized:

  P(C)=||M_C ψ||².

This is the general Born-type rule.

The rule follows from three candidate substrate properties:

1. quadratic weight;
2. additive weight for mutually exclusive relational classes;
3. normalization.

The unresolved issue is whether these three properties can themselves be derived uniquely from S∞↔B∞.

## 9. Measurement update

After an outcome C is observed, the accessible relational state is restricted to C.

The normalized post-measurement state is

  ψ' = M_C ψ / ||M_C ψ||.

Thus projection/update is interpreted as relational coarse-graining conditioned on the observed class.

This gives the standard projective measurement update without making collapse a separate physical force.

## 10. General observables

If a measurement has mutually exclusive outcome classes C_i with values λ_i, define

  A = Σ_i λ_i M_i.

Then

  <A> = Σ_i λ_i P_i
       = (ψ,Aψ)/(ψ,ψ).

Thus expectation values emerge from weighted relational projections.

The algebra M_i M_j=δ_ij M_i remains a structural condition on mutually exclusive coarse-grainings, not yet a theorem of the substrate.

## 11. What has actually been derived

### Strongly supported by the quadratic route

- continuous unit-circle geometry from normalized quadratic data;
- phase as the parameter of that geometry;
- complex amplitude as a compact representation;
- quadratic outcome weight;
- binary probabilities;
- interference cross terms;
- basis-dependent projection probabilities;
- normalized expectation values;
- conditional projection/update.

### Not yet uniquely derived

- arbitrary-dimensional complex state space;
- unique complex Hilbert-space structure;
- orthogonality from the substrate;
- all projector algebra;
- tensor-product composition;
- universal Born rule for every possible measurement;
- the physical meaning of the complex amplitudes.

## Verdict

**🟢 Strong structural derivation for the two-level/quadratic sector.**

**🟡 General quantum state space and measurement theory remain conditional extensions.**

The major result is that probability and measurement no longer need to be introduced as unrelated quantum axioms: they can be interpreted as quadratic coarse-graining of the same Meru relational structure.

The decisive next test is whether the same construction generalizes consistently to arbitrary dimensions and composite systems.
