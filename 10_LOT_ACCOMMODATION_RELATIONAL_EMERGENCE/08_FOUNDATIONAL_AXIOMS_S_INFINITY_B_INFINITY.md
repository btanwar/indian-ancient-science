## 2. Current foundational axioms

### Axiom A1 — Relational Configuration \(C\)

A physical relational system is represented at a given stage by a relational configuration:

\[
\boxed{C\in\mathcal C}
\]

where \(\mathcal C\) is the space of admissible relational configurations.

**Meaning:** The foundation begins with a configuration of relations rather than assuming coordinates, metric, particles, spacetime, or gravitational field as primitive objects.

**Why required:** Without a configuration/state domain there is nothing for a dynamical rule to act upon.

**What it does NOT assert:** It does not yet specify what the ultimate constituents of \(C\) are. Questions about whether “entities” themselves emerge are deliberately deferred.

**Reference:** PGA 1.12.0.1 — Foundational Primitive Reduction Test.

---

### Axiom A2 — Allowed Relational Transition \(T\)

A relational configuration can undergo an allowed transition:

\[
\boxed{T:C_i\rightarrow C_{i+1}}
\]

with the closure requirement

\[
C_i\in\mathcal C\Rightarrow T(C_i)\in\mathcal C.
\]

**Meaning:** The theory contains genuine change/reconfiguration rather than only a static relational description.

**Why required:** Without \(T\), there is no dynamics, ordered succession, propagation, or process to investigate.

**Critical limitation:** The form of \(T\) is currently unknown. Locality, conservation, reversibility, minimum change, elasticity, etc. have NOT been promoted to axioms merely because they are useful in toy models.

**Reference:** PGA 1.12.0.1 and PGA 1.12.0.2.

---

### Axiom A3 — Observable Distinction \(O\)

The relational configuration or its transition must admit a map to distinguishable information:

\[
\boxed{O:\mathcal C\rightarrow\mathcal O}
\]

or, operationally,

\[
O(C_i)\neq O(C_j)
\]

when the two configurations are physically distinguishable under the specified observation.

**Meaning:** The theory must provide a route from underlying relational states to distinguishable outcomes/information.

**Why required:** A theory with configurations and transitions but no operational distinction cannot yet connect its underlying dynamics to physical observables.

**Critical limitation:** \(O\) does not by itself specify an observer, measurement postulate, Born rule, or quantum measurement theory.

**Reference:** PGA 1.12.0.1 and the earlier Observable Reconstruction branch, especially PGA 1.12.2.2–1.12.2.8.

## 6. Foundational reasoning rule

The branch now follows a strict hierarchy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Axioms}
\rightarrow
\text{derived properties}
\rightarrow
\text{physical structures}
\rightarrow
\text{known-physics limits}
\rightarrow
\text{new predictions}
}
\]

A candidate property is promoted to an additional axiom only if:

1. it cannot be derived from the current axioms;
2. it is independently required for the intended theory;
3. the reason for adding it is explicit;
4. the addition is recorded as a new foundational decision rather than silently inserted into a toy model.

This prevents the research from becoming an unconstrained exercise in constructing increasingly elaborate relational systems.

### 10.3 Important classification rule

The matrix deliberately contains four different kinds of entries:

1. **Logical foundation** — required to formulate the research at all.
2. **Candidate foundational principle** — historically identified as potentially fundamental, but not yet derived/proven.
3. **Derived-property candidate** — should preferably emerge from the logical foundation and transition law.
4. **Benchmark / survival gate** — a requirement for comparison with known physics, not a microscopic axiom.

Therefore a row appearing in the matrix does **not** mean that it has been accepted as an axiom.
